# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

MC++ is developed in the open, by and for a research community.  Everyone who
takes part — reporting a problem, asking a question, reviewing a pull request,
or maintaining the library — is entitled to do so without harassment, and is
expected to extend the same to others.  This applies regardless of experience,
seniority, nationality, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, sexual
orientation, disability, personal appearance, religion, or any other personal
characteristic.

## What we expect

* Judge the work, not the person.  Technical disagreement is the normal way
  this field makes progress, and it is welcome.
* Say what is wrong and why.  "This enclosure is not valid for x < 0, see the
  case below" helps; "this is nonsense" does not.
* Assume the other person is acting in good faith, and ask before concluding
  otherwise.  Contributors here work in many languages and time zones.
* Accept that maintainers must weigh correctness, maintenance cost and scope,
  and may decline a contribution for reasons that have nothing to do with its
  author.

## What is not acceptable

* Personal attacks, insults, or derogatory remarks.
* Harassment of any kind, in public or in private, including unwelcome
  attention after a request to stop.
* Discriminatory language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances.
* Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
* Sustained disruption of discussions, issues, or review.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — the repository, its issues and pull
requests, and any other channel the project uses — and whenever someone is
representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report conduct that concerns you to the project lead, Benoit Chachuat
(<b.chachuat@imperial.ac.uk>).  Reports are read only by the maintainers
handling them, and are treated confidentially; you will get an acknowledgement
and, where you have asked for one, an account of the outcome.
You may report conduct directed at someone else, and you may report a
maintainer.

If the report concerns the project lead, or you would rather not write to a
maintainer, the Department of Chemical Engineering at Imperial College London
has its own reporting routes.

## What happens next

Maintainers will look into what was reported, and respond in proportion to it.
Depending on severity and on whether the behaviour continues, that may be a
private word, a public correction, removal of a comment or contribution, a
temporary block from the project's spaces, or a permanent one.  Maintainers who
do not uphold this code are subject to the same consequences as anyone else.
